# Dozens of open pixel fonts widen CJK

Yes: the CJK menu can grow well beyond Fusion Pixel's three plain-gothic sizes, and most of the growth is available under licenses that allow the app's convert-and-commit pipeline. The research found roughly 22 public-domain Japanese bitmap fonts at 12 to 24 px, a 15-face OFL Japanese series (KH Dot) with mincho, rounded, textbook and clerical styles at 12 to 32 px, about 20 OFL Chinese candidates (most of them 2025-2026 Chinese extensions of those same Japanese fonts, which finally give Chinese a Song, a rounded and several display faces at 16 px), and nine Korean families that add 15, 16 and 24 px plus bold and rounded 12 px styles. The sizes that open up are **14, 15, 16, 18, 20, 24 and 32 px**; the styles are serif (mincho/Song/myeongjo), rounded, bold, Fangsong, textbook, clerical and reverse-contrast display. Japanese is the richest and cleanest pool, Chinese has the most style variety but many unfinished fonts with Reserved Font Names, and Korean is the thinnest, with its only serif and handwriting bitmaps coming from a set with weak provenance. Things to reject are as clear as things to add: the whole HZK lineage (Vonwaon, IPix), the GPL WenQuanYi family, Nu mochi, Naga10, Elisa, DNF Bit Bit, Pix32 and DinkieBitmap all fail on license or provenance. Everything here rests on READMEs, license files and distributor pages: **no font file was opened**, so on-grid outlines, real glyph counts and the look of every font are unchecked, and a named list of candidates was never researched.

## Conversion makes every bundled font a Modified Version

The license test for this app is narrower than "free for commercial use". The app converts a source font to its own bitmap format and commits the result to a public repository, so a license must grant modification and redistribution of the modified file, not just use and embedding. Under SIL OFL 1.1 that is settled: the OFL FAQ says "a change in font format normally is considered modification", allows bundling Original and Modified Versions with software including "mobile device applications", and confines the OFL to the font data, leaving the app's own code and license untouched ([OFL FAQ](https://openfontlicense.org/ofl-faq/)). The obligations are the ones Fusion Pixel already imposes: keep the copyright notice and license text with the converted data and in the app, keep the converted data under OFL, never sell it alone. The FAQ also says artwork made with an OFL font belongs to its author, which matters for a tool whose users publish pixel art ([OFL FAQ](https://openfontlicense.org/ofl-faq/)).

The one new wrinkle is the **Reserved Font Name**. Fusion Pixel and Ark Pixel declare none ([Fusion LICENSE-OFL](https://github.com/TakWolf/fusion-pixel-font/blob/master/LICENSE-OFL)), but most of the Chinese candidates and two Korean ones do, and the FAQ says that with an RFN "you cannot use the Reserved Font Names in any way to identify the font to the user" in a Modified Version ([OFL FAQ](https://openfontlicense.org/ofl-faq/)). A converted font listed in the picker as "Mulmaru" or "长坂点宋 16" is exactly that case. The researchers' reading (not legal advice) is that each RFN font needs either a different display name or the author's written permission to keep the name. That is a per-font cost to weigh, not a blocker.

Three other license families behave differently. Public-domain and M+-licensed fonts carry no obligations at all: the M+ text grants "unlimited permission ... to use, copy, and distribute them, with or without modification, either commercially or noncommercially" ([M+ license in PixelMplus](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/itouhiro/PixelMplus/master/misc/mplus_bitmap_fonts/LICENSE_E)). GPL fonts are a poor fit: the FSF font exception covers only a "document" that embeds the font ([GNU GPL FAQ](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html#FontException)), and WenQuanYi's maintainers state that "using this GPL font in non-GPL monolithic software is a violation of the GPL license" ([wenq.org](http://wenq.org/wqy2/index.cgi?BitmapSong)). CC BY 4.0 works with attribution; CC BY-SA forces the converted data to stay BY-SA, and Creative Commons publishes no font-specific guidance on whether stamped text is an adaptation, so share-alike fonts are a worse fit for a drawing tool than OFL ones ([Creative Commons FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/)).

Case law on bitmap glyph shapes is permissive in the US, Japan and Korea, but it does not replace a license: a US regulation excludes "typeface as typeface" from copyright ([Copyright Office notice](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/compendium/ibr/ML-443.pdf)), Japan's Supreme Court refused copyright in a typeface in 2000 ([courts.go.jp](https://www.courts.go.jp/hanrei/54774/detail7/index.html)), yet Korea treats font files as protected programs and has protected typeface designs under design law since 2005 ([Korea Copyright Commission](https://www.copyright.or.kr/eng/doc/quarterly_pdf/123%20A%20Study%20on%20the%20Typeface%20Design%20Protection%20in%20Korea.pdf)). A public repo redistributing font data is the act licenses regulate, so fonts of unclear origin stay out regardless.

## Japanese has the deepest pool, and almost none of it overlaps Fusion

The single best index is the JF Dot page by 自家製フォント工房, which re-built X11-era bitmap fonts as TrueType with embedded bitmaps and tabulates their licenses. Its public-domain group is listed as unrestricted for commercial use, app embedding, modification and redistribution with no credit required, and every font covers kanji at least to JIS level 2 ([JF Dot fonts](http://jikasei.me/font/jf-dotfont/)). The second pillar is the **KH Dot series**: 15 typefaces designed by 平木敬太郎 in 1991-1996, once sold commercially and now released by the designer under OFL 1.1, all with JIS level 1 and 2 kanji ([KH Dot fonts](http://jikasei.me/font/kh-dotfont/)). Because the designer himself made the release, the commercial origin is not a provenance problem.

Three caveats shape the picks. First, the plain jiskan16 and jiskan24 builds carry Sony half-width glyphs that require a notice; the "jiskan16s" build avoids that with Shinonome half-widths, and jiskan24h swaps in the OFL Hibiya 24 ([JF Dot fonts](http://jikasei.me/font/jf-dotfont/)). Second, DenkiChip's author warns that many kanji are simplified shapes, unsuitable where exact strokes matter ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hicchicc/x8y12pxDenkiChip/main/README.md)). Third, the hicc display fonts (MaruMinya, MaruMonica, DonguriDuel, HeadUpDaisy, ScanLine, GridGazer and others) sit under a custom license that does allow processing, modification and redistribution while retaining copyright, and the author has announced OFL editions for all of them, with official distribution of the custom-license editions expected to end ([x0y0pxFreeFont](https://hicchicc.github.io/00ff/)). Waiting for each OFL edition is the tidy path.

Overlap with what already ships is minimal. Only Misaki is a declared Fusion upstream, supplying its 8 px Japanese kanji ([Fusion README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TakWolf/fusion-pixel-font/master/README.md)), so Misaki Gothic is redundant; Misaki Mincho changes only the non-kanji glyphs ([littlelimit.net Misaki](https://littlelimit.net/misaki.htm)). M+ BITMAP and PixelMplus are distinct designs but a second plain gothic at 10 and 12 px, so they rank below new sizes and styles.

The rejects are worth naming because two would have filled real gaps. **Naga10** is the only 10 px mincho and 10 px rounded design found, but commercial use requires consulting an author whose contact address has been dead for over 15 years; Elisa 8 and Pau16 are non-commercial only ([JF Dot fonts](http://jikasei.me/font/jf-dotfont/)). Whether a free app counts as commercial use is the owner's call, and nobody is left to ask. The Nu mochi fonts forbid redistribution, forbid distributing any derived data "usable like a font", and allow embedding only for fixed strings ([Nu mochi page](https://kokagem.sakura.ne.jp/font/mochi/)), which fails this app three ways.

The Chinese pool is newer and more style-driven. Its core is a wave of 2025-2026 OFL fonts that add Simplified and Traditional glyphs to the Japanese KH Dot, DotGothic16 and littlelimit fonts. The largest is the **yzdnn series** of nine fonts, each OFL 1.1 with its own Reserved Font Name ([yzdnn profile](https://github.com/yzdnn/yzdnn)).

The license chain for these derivatives holds up as far as primary pages go: the KH Dot originals are OFL per the distributor ([KH Dot fonts](http://jikasei.me/font/kh-dotfont/)), and the adapters state that their fonts keep that license. The authors' plain-language terms are explicit about what this app needs. FashionBitmap16, Cubic 11 and the Boutique fonts allow free commercial use without notice, bundling with software if OFL.txt is included, and modification under OFL without the reserved names ([FashionBitmap16](https://github.com/scott0107000/FashionBitmap16)); Z Labs adds an explicit right to embed in software and devices ([RoundPix 16px](https://github.com/Astro-2539/ZLabs-RoundPix-16px)). The practical weaknesses are completeness and naming. Five of the yzdnn fonts and RoundPix 16 call themselves unfinished, so a Text tool that ships them needs visible missing-glyph behavior and a plan to re-import releases, and nearly every one reserves its name.

Size coverage is lopsided. Variety exists almost only at 16 px, plus Song and rounded at 12 px. What look like 7, 9 and 11 px Chinese fonts are 8, 10 and 12 px cells under another name, and the only Chinese fonts found at true 13 to 15 px are GPL WenQuanYi. No clean-licensed pixel Kai was found; Kai survives only in legacy ROM and system fonts catalogued by a specimen archive that publishes no font files ([chinese-bitmap-specimens](https://github.com/legendtired/chinese-bitmap-specimens)). Fusion's own upstreams (Cubic 11, BoutiqueBitmap 7x7 and 9x9, MisekiBitmap) are separate designs only inside their own script range, so the one clear addition among them is Boutique 9x9's drawn Bold, which Fusion does not carry.

Provenance draws a hard line through the older Chinese fonts. Everything built on HZK12/HZK16 fails: TakWolf's own conversion opens by warning that the font "is not public domain" and that users bear the copyright risk, with the glyphs variously traced to UCDOS, early WPS or Hong Kong's Compufont ([hzk-pixel-font](https://github.com/pixel-font-legacy/hzk-pixel-font)). Vonwaon Bitmap wears a CC0 label, but its author concedes the copyright risk and advises users with strict requirements to avoid HZK-based projects ([Vonwaon](https://timothyqiu.itch.io/vonwaon-bitmap)); commenters on IPix trace the same glyphs to a Beijing company still in the bitmap-font business ([IPix](https://purestudio.itch.io/ipix)). Guanzhi 8px, Zfull, "xiaodianzhen" and Windows XP extractions are likewise unlicensed, and **ChillBitmap 7px admits to containing a few Guanzhi glyphs** ([ChillBitmap](https://github.com/Warren2060/ChillBitmap)), which taints it until those glyphs are identified. The GPL family (WenQuanYi Bitmap Song at 12 to 16 px, Uranus Pixel, Nightgazer, Dotted fonts) is closed by its maintainers' own reading of the GPL, and WenQuanYi's README tells commercial users to contact the maintainer ([WenQuanYi README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AmusementClub/WenQuanYi-Bitmap-Song-TTF/master/Original%20Fonts/README)). Paid fonts stay out: Pix32 charges USD 200 for commercial products and says nothing about modification ([Pix32](https://github.com/32comic/Pix32)).

Galmuri needs care with names: the number is not the pixel size (Galmuri7 is 8 px, Galmuri9 is 10 px, Galmuri11 is 12 px, Galmuri14 is 15 px), and the three regular faces at 8, 10 and 12 px are already inside Fusion Pixel ([Galmuri README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/quiple/galmuri/main/README.md)). So the new Galmuri material is Galmuri14 and the Bold and Condensed cuts of Galmuri11.

Provenance risk in Korean scales with how retro the font looks. The DOS set is the only free source of myeongjo and handwriting bitmaps, and its author says plainly that the fonts were made "by extracting bitmap data", with one taken from the game 삼국지3; the MIT label is applied by the extractor, who has stopped maintaining the set ([hurss fonts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hurss/fonts/master/README.md)). Style gain and risk arrive together, and the report's position is to leave that set out. Mulmaru sits in the middle: its README says the Hangul "mainly referenced" the commercial Yoon px 아케이드 and asserts compliance with Korean copyright and design law ([Mulmaru README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mushsooni/mulmaru/main/README.md)). HBIOS-SYS brings a share-alike term through its Latin glyphs; converting only its OFL Hangul and pairing another Latin avoids that ([HBIOS-SYS](https://hbios.quiple.dev/)). The corporate pattern is the one to watch for: DNF Bit Bit allows commercial use and bundling but forbids redistributing a modified font file, which rules out committed converted data ([Sandoll Cloud](https://www.sandollcloud.com/free-font/19491/DNF-Bit-Bit-v2)).

## Unifont is still the only complete 16 px pan-CJK font

No new open pan-CJK pixel font above 12 px exists outside GNU Unifont. Ark Pixel deprecated and removed its 16 px size after the 2026.09.01 build, because it "actually contained only a small number of hanzi", and its author now points users to Unifont ([Ark 16px archive](https://github.com/pixel-font-studio/ark-pixel-font-16px-glyphs-archive/blob/master/README.md)). Ark's 10 and 12 px are what Fusion is built on, so shipping Ark adds no new look. TakWolf's one new design, the rounded 12 px Jelly Pixel Font, is a proof of concept with 394 of 20,992 unified ideographs ([Jelly info](https://github.com/TakWolf/jelly-pixel-font/blob/master/docs/info-12px-proportional.md)). Fusion Bold is an algorithmic shift-and-overlap bold that its own author does not recommend ([Fusion Bold](https://github.com/pixel-font-studio/fusion-bold-pixel-font/blob/master/README.md)).

Unifont deserves a second look on facts that have changed, though its Song-like plainness has not. It is **dual-licensed OFL 1.1 or GPLv2+ with the font embedding exception**, its maintainers state outright that it can be used with non-free software, and version 18.0.01 of September 2026 ships three regional flavors as BDF: default (WenQuanYi-derived mainland forms), `unifont_jp` with complete JIS X 0213 coverage, and `unifont_t`, a set of over 28,000 Taiwan-form glyphs added in March 2026 ([unifoundry.com](https://unifoundry.com/unifont/index.html)). Those flavors map onto the app's existing Japanese, Simplified and Traditional split. Under the OFL option the obligations equal Fusion's. The decision remains aesthetic, and the single-language 16 px fonts above now offer better-looking alternatives for Japanese and Chinese, which leaves Unifont's strongest role as the complete fallback rather than a headline face.

The two multi-script indie fonts are weaker. LanaPixel is designed for 11 px with about 19,400 characters and is offered under CC BY 4.0 or OFL, but its author says many CJK characters are nearly illegible at that size, and its primary pages could not be read ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/node/114657)). Silver pairs a CC BY 4.0 grant with a request to buy a license above a USD 100,000 budget, states no native pixel size, and has user reports of missing glyphs ([Silver](https://poppyworks.itch.io/silver)). Neither separates Japanese, Chinese and Korean glyph forms.

One finding about the font already shipped: Fusion's 10 px is its weak size for Chinese, with GB 2312 level 2 at 2,190 of 3,008 and Big5 less-common at 2,168 of 7,652 ([Fusion 10 px info](https://github.com/TakWolf/fusion-pixel-font/blob/master/docs/info-10px-proportional.md)), while 8 px and 12 px are near complete.

## What was not verified, and what was not researched

**No font file was downloaded or opened.** Every statement about formats, glyph counts, embedded bitmap strikes and pixel sizes repeats an author's or distributor's page. Whether any TTF's outlines sit exactly on the pixel grid is unmeasured for every font; only DenkiChip, DenkiChip Hangul, the JF Dot and KH Dot builds and the TakWolf and Unifont releases state bitmap strikes or BDF. No specimen image was viewed, so every style description is the author's wording and "visibly different" is unconfirmed by eye. Exact glyph counts are missing for most Japanese fonts (pages say "JIS X 0208" or "level 1 and 2"), for every yzdnn font, for NeoDunggeunmo, Galmuri14's Hangul and Unifont's flavors.

License verification has specific holes. Public-domain status for the Japanese fonts other than Shinonome rests on JF Dot's table, not on each original archive; the original BDF download locations were not followed and some 2000-era hosts may be gone. Whether KH Dot or DotGothic16 declare a Reserved Font Name was not checked. Most Chinese licenses were taken from README statements and GitHub's detection, with only Cubic 11's and MuzaiPixel's license files opened; GitHub reports no detected license for ConcisePixel 10. The notes conflict on hicc's MaruMinya: the author's site lists only DenkiChip, NegaTape and NegaClip as OFL editions ([x0y0pxFreeFont](https://hicchicc.github.io/00ff/)), while the Z Labs README says MaruMinya gained an OFL license in February 2026 ([RoundPix 12px](https://github.com/Astro-2539/ZLabs-RoundPix-12px)); this affects Z Labs RoundPix 12px and MaruMinya Hangul and should be settled at the source. Two smaller conflicts stand: the efont page lists no 14 px Shinonome mincho although JF Dot ships one, and a 2015 review gives KH Dot's largest size as 36 dots against the distributor's 32. The LanaPixel, Silver, DNF Bit Bit, 横浜ドット and DinkieBitmap terms come from secondary pages or search summaries. The public-domain claim for NeoDunggeunmo's source font is second-hand. Whether adapters drew new hanzi themselves or copied shapes from other bitmap fonts cannot be told from READMEs, and no primary source was found on the usage terms of Chinese national-standard bitmap fonts. The CC BY legal code and OpenType bitmap-table details in the notes were written from memory, not re-fetched.

Candidates named but **not researched**: for Japanese, Nosutaru-dot, Enka Dot Gothic 24, Mona 12 Text JP, かたすみドット, 触手のしょほんと, KFひま字, and the kanji coverage of most hicc fonts. For Chinese, DWNfonts' QuanPixel, Fonsung, Chusung, Lyusung (24 px), CLFN and PoxiaoPixel, "12×16莫妮卡圆像素", UwU Matrix, ChillPixels, the X11 taipei16/24 originals behind the CC BY-SA Taipei Fonts, the X11 gb16/gb24 and Arphic bitmaps, ETen fonts, Minecraft-style Chinese fonts, and anything hosted on Gitee. For Korean, Pixelroborobo, Stardust, the original DungGeunMo distribution, Hanterm and X11 hanglm16/24, Baekmuk and Daewoo bitmaps, 도깨비 fonts, Puzzle Sans, MapleSaemmul, font-korean-7x7, IyagiGGC's license section, and burpyfresh's itch.io fonts. Across scripts, GNU intlfonts, wqy-unibit, Kreative and BabelStone fonts, and general "awesome pixel fonts" lists were not checked, and the regional "free font" clause patterns were not studied from primary sources.

## Conclusion

The thin CJK menu reflects where the research stopped at Fusion Pixel, not what exists. A 1990s Japanese designer releasing his commercial bitmap series under OFL turned out to be the keystone: KH Dot feeds Japanese directly and, through the yzdnn and scott0107000 adaptations, supplies most of the Chinese style variety, so one provenance chain underwrites two languages. Korean has no equivalent keystone, which is why its only serif and handwriting faces are extractions. The Chinese scene is also moving fast enough that a search repeated in a few months will find more, and several of today's unfinished fonts will have matured.

The work that remains is inspection, not discovery. Opening the files settles the three unknowns this report cannot: on-grid outlines or BDF availability, real glyph counts, and whether each face looks good enough to ship. The naming decision for Reserved Font Name fonts is the one policy question that applies to nearly every Chinese pick and two Korean ones, and answering it once (rename, or ask the authors) unlocks the whole group.
